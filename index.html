Your job: engineer one rescue robot

A warehouse fire has cut off a field clinic. Your team must design one robot that sorts medical supplies, drives safely past obstacles, and carries the supplies to the clinic before its battery runs low. You will build the robot up in three missions.

The three missions

1Precision Sorting: choose a motor, gear ratio and arm length so the arm can lift every pack (torque) without moving too fast.

2Obstacle Avoidance: choose a sensor and a speed so the robot can always stop before it hits something (stopping distance).

3Rescue Run: choose sensor, speed and load so the robot delivers all supplies fast while keeping 20% battery (energy and power).

How to work like an engineer

  1. Read the mission brief. The fixed values are your constraints.
  2. Predict, then press Run test. Every test is saved in your trial table.
  3. Change one setting at a time (fair test) and watch what changes.
  4. The starting design always fails. Find a design that passes, then find the best one.
  5. The best passing trial in each mission gets a ★ in the table.
  6. Export your trials (CSV) when you finish.

Key physics you will use

τ = F × r
output torque = motor torque × gear ratio × efficiency
v = ω × r

stopping distance = vi × treaction + vi2 ÷ (2a)
a = F ÷ m

P = F × v
E = P × t
P = V × I
battery energy (Wh) = V × Ah

The lab uses a simplified engineering model. Real robots also deal with friction changes, motor heating and sensor noise, but the trade-offs are real.

Model notes (motors)

Each motor is used at its rated point: it gives its rated torque while turning at its rated speed. A real DC motor has a torque–speed line: it spins faster when lightly loaded and slows down as the load approaches stall. Using the rated point keeps the model simple and safe, because the arm never needs more than the rated torque.

Model notes (resistive forces)

Driving force needed F = rolling resistance + terrain drag = 0.06 × m × g + 0.8 × v². Drive efficiency is 70%, so drive power = F × v ÷ 0.70. Sensor power: IR 0.5 W, ultrasonic 1.0 W, LiDAR 8.0 W.

Faster saves time but costs energy. Heavier loads mean fewer trips but longer stops and more current.
